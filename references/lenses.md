# Thinking lenses

Use this reference for every nontrivial reframing. Its purpose is to prevent premature convergence on the first plausible interpretation.

Select 3–6 lenses that create competing hypotheses, normally from at least three different families. Generate one or more candidate questions from each useful lens, then compare the candidates by goal leverage. Lens count and candidate count are different: if the selected lenses produce fewer than five genuinely competing questions, develop additional hypotheses or candidate questions before ranking. Do not mechanically apply every model, combine framework names in the output, or prefer a lens merely because it sounds sophisticated.

## Goal and value

| Lens | What it tests | Candidate direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First principles | What fundamentally determines the outcome once conventions and inherited assumptions are removed? | Which condition must be true for the desired result to exist at all? |
| Jobs To Be Done | What progress is the person actually trying to make in a specific situation? | What change is the proposed solution being hired to create? |
| Opportunity cost | What valuable alternative, time, attention, or flexibility is displaced? | What are we giving up by continuing down this path? |

## Causality and truth

| Lens | What it tests | Candidate direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Causal thinking | Which variables produce the outcome rather than merely correlate with it? | What evidence would distinguish the suspected cause from a competing cause? |
| Counterfactual | Whether the outcome would still occur without the suspected factor | If this factor disappeared, would the problem remain? |
| Inversion | What would reliably prevent or destroy success? | What failure condition matters more than the proposed improvement? |

## Constraints and leverage

| Lens | What it tests | Candidate direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theory of constraints | Which current bottleneck limits the whole outcome? | What single constraint makes improvement elsewhere mostly irrelevant? |
| 80/20 | Which small number of causes explain most of the result? | Which few inputs account for most of the gap? |
| Systems thinking | Where interactions, feedback loops, delays, or leverage points dominate | What feedback loop keeps recreating the problem? |
| Funnel | At which stage the largest meaningful loss occurs | Where do suitable people, attempts, or value drop out most? |
| Marginal thinking | Where the next unit of effort produces the greatest incremental return | Where would one more unit of effort matter most now? |

## Decisions and consequences

| Lens | What it tests | Candidate direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expected value | Which uncertainty most changes the probability-weighted outcome? | Which unknown could reverse the preferred choice? |
| Risk | Which probability-times-impact combination dominates the downside? | What plausible failure would be unacceptable even if unlikely? |
| Reversibility | Which choice is costly or difficult to undo? | What must be learned before making the least reversible move? |
| Second-order effects | What follows after the immediate visible result? | If this works initially, what new behavior or constraint appears next? |

## Behavior and incentives

| Lens | What it tests | Candidate direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incentives | What behavior the actual rewards, costs, status, or rules produce | What are people rewarded for doing, regardless of stated intent? |

## Selection discipline

Start with lenses that could disagree with one another. A strong default is:

1. one goal or value lens
2. one causal or counterfactual lens
3. one constraint or system lens
4. one decision, risk, or consequence lens
5. an incentives lens when other people or repeated behavior materially shape the outcome

If all candidates support the user's initial theory, deliberately choose a lens that could falsify it. If every candidate is abstract, add a lens that locates a present constraint or learnable unknown. If every candidate is tactical, add a lens that tests the real outcome or causal model.

Stop adding lenses when new candidates no longer reveal a different decision-relevant unknown. The model inventory exists to deepen the search; the final output is still one plain-language question.
